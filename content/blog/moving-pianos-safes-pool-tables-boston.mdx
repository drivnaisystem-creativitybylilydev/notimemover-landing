Short answer: a piano runs $275-$650 to move locally, a gun safe $250-$600, and a pool table $300-$800 — and Boston's stairs push every one of those numbers higher. Here's what each item actually costs, and what changes once you're carrying it up three flights instead of rolling it out a ground-floor door.

## The Real Cost to Move Heavy and Specialty Items

Most people get a number in their head from a friend's move or a quick search, then find out their own quote looks nothing like it. That's usually because the number they saw didn't account for stairs, distance, or which specific version of the item they own. Here's the baseline before any of that gets added.

| Item | Local Move Cost | Long-Distance Cost | What Pushes It Up |
|---|---|---|---|
| Upright piano | $100-$350 | $450-$1,200 | Stairs (~$100/flight), narrow doorways |
| Grand piano | $250-$650 | $1,200-$3,000 | Size, disassembling the legs/lid, stairs |
| Gun safe / heavy vault | $250-$600 | Priced by weight | Weight over 600 lbs, stairs, tight turns |
| Pool table (slate) | $300-$800 | $600-$4,000 | Disassembly/reassembly, slate handling |

These ranges hold up across [HomeGuide's 2026 piano moving data](https://homeguide.com/costs/piano-moving-cost) and industry pricing for safes and pool tables. Your actual number lands somewhere in the range depending on three things: the item's weight, how far the crew has to carry it, and whether stairs are involved. In Boston, that third factor is doing more work than it does almost anywhere else.

## How Much Does It Cost to Move a Piano in Boston?

An upright piano averages $275 for a local move; a grand piano averages closer to $450, per HomeGuide. Those are flat-rate averages assuming reasonably easy access — ground floor, no long carry, no stairs.

Add stairs and the number moves fast. Piano movers typically add about $100 per flight, and a piano needs a piano board, straps, and at least two people trained to use them, not just extra hands. That's a meaningfully different job than carrying a couch, because a piano's weight is concentrated and off-balance in a way that punishes a rushed crew.

This is where Boston's housing stock actually matters. A third-floor walk-up in Somerville or Jamaica Plain isn't a rare case here, it's the default. If you're moving a piano as part of a [local move](/local-moving) out of (or into) a [triple-decker without an elevator](/blog/hardest-boston-neighborhoods-to-move), plan on the higher end of the range and confirm your mover has actually moved a piano before, not just furniture.

## What About Gun Safes and Heavy Vaults?

Gun safes and home vaults get priced differently than furniture because weight, not size, drives the job. A standard safe move runs $250-$600 locally, and most movers add a flat specialty item fee of $100-$300 on top of regular labor, separate from any stair charge.

Once a safe crosses roughly 600 pounds, or the move involves stairs, the total can climb to $600-$900, according to [Liberty Safe's own pricing breakdown](https://libertysafe.com/blogs/the-vault/how-much-does-it-cost-to-deliver-and-move-a-gun-safe). That's not a markup for the sake of it — a safe that heavy needs a stair-rated hand truck, furniture straps rated for the load, and usually a three- or four-person crew instead of two. Trying to muscle a 500-pound safe down a narrow triple-decker staircase without the right equipment is how staircases, walls, and backs get damaged in that order.

If your safe is bolted to the floor, mention that when you book too. Unbolting adds time the crew needs to plan for, not discover.

## Pool Tables Need Disassembly, Not Muscle

A pool table move runs $300-$800 locally, including disassembly, transport, and reassembly or releveling at the new place, per [Angi's 2026 pool table cost data](https://www.angi.com/articles/how-much-cost-move-pool-table.htm). The number climbs to $600-$4,000 for long-distance moves, largely because of the extra handling and protection the slate needs over distance.

The order matters here: pockets and rails come off first, then the felt, then the slate gets unbolted from the frame in sections. Each slate section gets wrapped separately, because a cracked slate is the single most common way a pool table move goes wrong — and slate isn't a part you can easily replace. Reassembly is the reverse, plus releveling once the table's on the new floor, since an unlevel table is basically unusable.

If your table has a one-piece slate bed rather than three sections, or your crew tells you they'll "just carry it as-is," that's worth pushing back on. A table worth more than $1,000, or one with sentimental value, is worth paying a company that's disassembled slate before.

## Why Boston's Walk-Ups Change the Math

Everything above assumes reasonably normal access. Boston rarely offers that. Triple-deckers, brownstones, and pre-war walk-ups mean stairs are the rule, not the exception, and a piano or safe moves at a fraction of the speed a couch does on the same staircase.

Tight turns matter as much as the number of flights. A grand piano's legs come off for a reason, and a safe that won't clear a landing turn needs a different route planned before the crew shows up, not improvised in the stairwell. Streets like Beacon Hill's or the North End's, [where a full-size truck can't always reach the door](/blog/hardest-boston-neighborhoods-to-move), add a longer carry on top of the stairs — and a longer carry with a 400-pound safe is a very different ask than the same carry with a box of books.

Crews we work with size up a piano or safe the same way they size up a third-floor walk-up: before the quote, not after the truck is parked. A heavy or awkward item changes the labor and equipment needed, and that has to get priced upfront. It's the same principle behind [locking your budget in before move day](/blog/hidden-moving-fees-boston) — a specialty item fee sprung on you at the door is exactly the kind of surprise that model is built to avoid.

## Does Your Mover's Insurance Actually Cover These Items?

Almost certainly not enough. Every Massachusetts mover carries released value protection by default, which pays out at $0.60 per pound, per item — not what the item is worth. A 300-pound upright piano damaged in transit gets you $180. A gun safe worth $2,000 gets treated the same way a 300-pound box of tools would.

For anything in this category, [full value protection](/blog/moving-insurance-boston) is worth the upgrade. It reimburses at repair, replacement, or market value instead of a per-pound formula, and it's the only coverage that actually reflects what a piano, safe, or pool table is worth. Ask for the upgrade cost as a specific dollar number tied to your inventory, not a vague percentage quoted over the phone.

## Do You Need a Specialty Mover, or Will Regular Movers Do?

Most general movers can handle all three of these items competently, as long as they have the right equipment and disclose that upfront. A piano board and straps, a stair-rated hand truck, and a crew that's disassembled a pool table before are the real requirements, not a separate "specialty" business card.

Where it's worth paying for a dedicated specialty company: a concert-grade piano headed into climate-controlled storage, safes over roughly 800 pounds, or slate pool tables where your regular mover admits they haven't disassembled one. If you're moving into a high-rise, this is also where you'll need to coordinate a [certificate of insurance for the freight elevator](/blog/boston-high-rise-moving-elevator-coi) well before move day, since a 400-pound safe almost always requires the freight elevator rather than the passenger one.

## What to Tell Your Movers Before You Book

The quote you get is only as accurate as the information you give it. Before booking, have these answers ready:

- The item's approximate weight, or the make and model so the mover can look it up
- Number of flights of stairs on both ends, and whether either building has an elevator
- Doorway and stairwell width, especially for a grand piano or one-piece slate table
- Whether the item needs full disassembly (pool tables, most pianos with removable legs)
- Whether your building requires a COI for elevator or loading dock access

Movers who ask you these questions during booking, rather than finding out on move day, are the ones who quote accurately the first time.
