Free moving boxes are everywhere in Boston if you know which doors to knock on. Here's where to actually find them, which ones to skip, and how many you need before you start.

## How Many Boxes Do You Actually Need?

Figure this out before you start collecting, or you'll end up with a stack of mismatched boxes that's either way too many or nowhere near enough. Per [moveBuddha's packing calculator](https://www.movebuddha.com/packing-moving-box-calculator/), a studio or one-bedroom apartment typically runs 30-50 boxes, and a two-bedroom lands between 50-80. Add a 10-20% buffer for anything you're not thinking about yet, closets always hold more than people expect.

| Apartment Size | Boxes Needed |
|---|---|
| Studio | 20-30 |
| 1BR | 30-50 |
| 2BR | 50-80 |
| 3BR or house | 80-120+ |

These numbers assume a mix of sizes, small boxes for books and dishes, medium and large for clothes and linens, so don't collect 50 identical medium boxes and call it done. You'll want a real spread, plus a handful of wardrobe boxes if you're not disassembling your closet by hand.

If you're not sure your count is even close, run a quick pass through each room before you start hunting for boxes. A lot of Boston apartments carry more than the layout suggests, especially studios and one-bedrooms that have been lived in for a few years. [Decluttering before you pack](/blog/declutter-before-a-boston-move) usually cuts the total box count by a noticeable amount, and it's a lot easier to donate a box of stuff than to carry it up three flights twice.

## Liquor Stores Are Your Best Free Moving Box Source

Liquor stores get shipments constantly, and the boxes they receive are built to carry glass bottles without collapsing. That makes them noticeably sturdier than a typical grocery box, and a lot of them come with cardboard dividers already built in, which happen to be perfect for wrapping stemware, picture frames, or anything else that shouldn't rattle around in transit.

Most liquor stores stack their empties out back specifically because people ask for them constantly around move dates. Call ahead, ask what time they break down shipments, and show up with a car. Package stores in dense neighborhoods like Allston, Brighton, and Mission Hill tend to go through the most volume, so they're a reliable stop if you're packing near a college area.

## What About Grocery Stores and Big-Box Retailers?

Supermarkets are the next-easiest stop. Dry goods boxes, cereal cartons, canned-good cases, hold up well and are usually free for the asking. Skip the produce boxes if you can avoid them; they've often sat in a damp cooler and can carry a lingering smell into your closed-up apartment. Ask an employee rather than digging through the recycling area yourself, most stores keep a stack set aside once they know people are asking.

Costco and Sam's Club are worth a stop too, since they break down large shipments daily and often leave flattened boxes by the exit. Bookstores are another good source if you're near one. The boxes publishers ship books in are dense and reinforced, which makes them genuinely good for the heaviest stuff you own: books, kitchen items, tools. It's worth calling ahead to ask, since it depends on what came in that week.

None of these stops will hand you a box built for stemware or a framed mirror, though. Fragile items still need actual packing materials, not just a sturdy box, so if your move includes glassware, art, or electronics, budget for [proper wrapping and cushioning](/blog/packing-fragile-items-boston-move) on top of whatever you collect for free. A free box with no padding inside it is just a box that breaks something on the ride over.

## Buy Nothing Boston and Other Local Groups

Online giveaway groups are where a lot of Boston's free-box supply actually moves. [Buy Nothing Boston](https://www.boston.com/community/readers-say/buy-nothing-groups-help-readers-give-and-take-for-free/) has grown to close to 21,000 members since it started, and box posts show up constantly, especially right after someone else finishes their own move and wants the pile gone. Neighborhood-specific groups (Everything Free Allston/Brighton is one example) work the same way on a smaller scale.

Facebook Marketplace and Nextdoor both have a "free" filter that's worth checking daily for a week or two before your move. The catch with all of these: timing matters more than searching. Post your own request too, not just browsing what's listed, since plenty of people moving out would rather hand off a stack of clean boxes than break them down for recycling.

## When Free Moving Boxes Aren't Worth the Time

Free boxes cost you something even when no money changes hands: time, and a car to haul them in. If you're packing an entire two-bedroom in the four days before [September 1st](/blog/moving-september-1-boston), or you're working around a job that doesn't leave evenings free, chasing mismatched free boxes across three neighborhoods isn't actually saving you anything.

A box kit from U-Haul or a similar supplier costs more than free, but not much. Here's roughly what you're looking at per box, based on [U-Haul's current pricing](https://www.uhaul.com/MovingSupplies/Boxes/Standard-Sized-Moving-Boxes/Medium-Moving-Box/?id=2793):

| Box Type | Typical Cost |
|---|---|
| Small box | $1.50-$2 |
| Medium box | $2-$3 |
| Large box | $3-$5 |
| Wardrobe box | $17-$18 |

For a one-bedroom's worth of boxes bought new, that's usually somewhere in the $80-$150 range depending on how many wardrobe boxes you add. Plastic bin rental services have also shown up in Boston in the last couple years, delivered and picked up for you, which is worth a look if you'd rather not deal with cardboard at all.

We see this tradeoff play out on plenty of Boston jobs: people spend two weekends collecting free boxes to save $60-$100, then end up stressed and under-packed the night before the crew shows up. If your move date is locked in and your time is actually worth something, buying a clean set is the better trade. One upside of booking a [local move](/local-moving) with a company that locks your price upfront is that a box run doesn't turn into a surprise line item either way, it's a separate decision you make on your own timeline, not something that gets folded into a shifting quote.

## What Do You Do With the Boxes After?

Nobody plans for this part, and then moving day ends with a mountain of cardboard in the living room. [Boston's recycling program](https://www.boston.gov/departments/public-works/recycling-boston) takes cardboard curbside, but it needs to be flattened first, clean and dry, with no tape, styrofoam, or packing peanuts mixed in. If a box is too big to fit in your bin even flattened, break it down further before you set it out rather than leaving it whole next to the bin.

Before you recycle anything, though, consider passing boxes along instead. The same Buy Nothing groups that supplied your boxes are full of people about to start their own move, and a stack of clean, already-broken-down boxes is genuinely useful to the next person. It's the same system running in the other direction.

If you packed your kitchen using a [size-specific packing plan](/blog/packing-guide-boston-apartment), you likely have a decent mix of small and medium boxes left over in good shape, worth keeping a few flattened in a closet for the next time you need to ship something or store off-season gear, rather than breaking every single one down for recycling right away.

## The Bottom Line

Free moving boxes in Boston are real and easy to find, liquor stores and grocery stores first, Buy Nothing groups second, bookstores as a bonus if you're near one. Just budget the time it takes to collect them, and know when buying a matching set is worth the money instead. Either way, count what you actually need before you start, using the table above as your starting point rather than guessing and hoping.
