Massachusetts banned mattresses from the trash back in 2022. If yours is heading out the door before your move, here's exactly how to get rid of it without a fine, a wasted trip, or a mattress still sitting in the hallway on moving day.

## Why You Can't Just Trash a Mattress in Massachusetts Anymore

This isn't a Boston-specific quirk. It's state law. Starting November 1, 2022, [MassDEP expanded its waste disposal ban](https://www.mass.gov/guides/mattress-recycling) to cover mattresses and box springs, meaning landfills, transfer stations, and waste-to-energy facilities across Massachusetts stopped accepting them entirely. Put one out with your regular trash and it simply won't get taken.

The reasoning tracks with why the ban exists in the first place. Mattresses are bulky, expensive to transport, and they chew up landfill space fast — more than 600,000 get thrown away in Massachusetts every year. Worse, springs and metal frames can jam or damage incinerator equipment at waste-to-energy plants. MassDEP's own numbers put the recoverable share at over 75% of a mattress's components, foam, fabric, and steel included, which is the whole argument for recycling over dumping.

None of that matters much when you're the one standing in an empty bedroom with a queen mattress and a move-out date. What matters is knowing your actual options, and Boston's are better than most cities.

## Boston's Free Curbside Pickup

If your building has six units or fewer, the city will take your mattress for free. [Boston's mattress recycling program](https://www.boston.gov/departments/public-works/mattress-recycling), booked through 311, covers memory foam, innerspring, hybrid, latex, and box springs, up to 10 items per calendar year per address.

Scheduling is the part people underestimate. Slots fill up during the late-summer moving rush, especially anywhere near September 1, so book the appointment as soon as you know your mattress is going, not the week before you move. Put it out the night before your scheduled date, not earlier — an early mattress on the curb is an open invitation for scavengers, weather damage, or a bulk-trash violation if it sits too long unclaimed.

One detail worth knowing: the program doesn't require a specific mattress bag the way some cities do. Wrapping it in plastic sheeting anyway is still a smart move. It keeps the mattress dry if rain hits before pickup, and it's the same habit you'd want if you're carrying it down a stairwell yourself first.

## What If Your Building Has 7 or More Units?

Boston's free program stops at six units. Above that, you're not covered by 311, and the responsibility usually shifts to the building itself.

Start with your property manager or landlord. Larger apartment buildings and condo associations often already have a hauling contract in place for exactly this situation, and skipping that step means paying for something the building may already handle. If there's no existing arrangement, a private mattress recycler is the next call, and most operate in the same 24-72 hour window a moving company would.

This is the same category of building that usually requires a Certificate of Insurance before a freight elevator gets reserved for [move day itself](/blog/boston-high-rise-moving-elevator-coi), so it's worth checking both requirements at once with the front desk or the management office. A building that manages elevator bookings tightly tends to manage bulk-item disposal the same way, with its own rules about where and when items can leave through the loading dock rather than the front lobby.

## Comparing Your Mattress Disposal Options

| Method | Cost | Typical Timeline | Best For |
|---|---|---|---|
| City curbside pickup (311) | Free | Book 1-2 weeks ahead, especially near Sept. 1 | Buildings with 6 units or fewer |
| Private mattress recycler or junk hauler | Roughly $30-$100 per mattress | Often next-day to a few days out | 7+ unit buildings or a tight move timeline |
| Charity donation pickup | Free, often tax-deductible | 1-2 weeks lead time; mattress must pass inspection | Mattresses in genuinely good, unstained condition |
| New mattress delivery haul-away | Often free to ~$50 with the delivery | Same day as your new mattress arrives | Anyone replacing the mattress they're getting rid of |

The fastest wrong move is assuming one of these applies without checking first. A "free" charity pickup that gets cancelled at the door because of a stain costs you more time than just booking 311 from the start.

## Can You Donate an Old Mattress Instead?

Donation is the better outcome when the mattress still has real life left in it, but charities are pickier about mattresses than almost anything else they accept. Stains, tears, odors, or a mattress much past 7-8 years old will usually get turned away on the spot, since most donation centers can't legally resell a mattress that fails a basic hygiene and safety check.

Call ahead before you load anything into a car or count on a pickup. Ask directly whether they take mattresses at all, since plenty of general donation centers stopped accepting them years ago over exactly this liability. If the mattress genuinely qualifies, donation is free and often comes with a receipt for a tax deduction, which beats paying a hauler for something that still had years of use left in it.

A mattress protector or a cover you've kept clean for the life of the mattress makes the difference here more than people expect. It's the easiest way to prove condition on the spot instead of arguing about it at the door.

## What Happens If You Skip the Pickup Appointment?

Leaving a mattress on the curb without a scheduled pickup isn't a shortcut, it's a risk that can turn into a fine. Boston's special-collection rules exist because unscheduled bulk items left on the street create a real cleanup problem for the city, and mattresses specifically are heavy, awkward, and expensive for public works crews to move twice. An unclaimed mattress with no 311 appointment tied to it is exactly the kind of illegal dumping the city's rules are written to catch.

The workaround some people try, informal curb "free" culture around September 1 when half the city turns over leases at once, doesn't really apply here the way it does for a lamp or a bookshelf. Very few people are willing to take a stranger's used mattress off the sidewalk, bed bug risk being the obvious reason, so it tends to sit there far longer than furniture does. Booking the actual pickup remains the faster option even when it feels like more effort upfront.
