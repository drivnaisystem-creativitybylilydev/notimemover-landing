Your business doesn't get a moving day off. Here's how to relocate a Boston office without losing a week of billable hours to it.

## Start With the Building, Not the Boxes

Home moves in Boston run into freight elevators and Certificates of Insurance often enough, but a commercial office move runs into them almost every time. Downtown towers in the Seaport, Back Bay, and the Financial District manage their own loading docks and freight elevators the same way [managed residential buildings do](/blog/boston-high-rise-moving-elevator-coi), except commercial landlords are typically stricter about it. Reservation windows tend to run in narrow blocks, sometimes as short as 30 minutes per trip, and property managers commonly want 48 hours' notice minimum before they'll confirm one.

The COI itself asks more of a commercial mover than a residential one. Office buildings with server rooms or executive suites sometimes push past the standard $1 million general liability minimum, and they'll want the certificate holder's exact legal name, which is often a management company rather than the building's name on the lobby directory. Get this wrong and you're re-submitting paperwork instead of moving desks. Before you book anyone, confirm your mover is [fully insured](/blog/what-fully-insured-means-boston-movers) and can turn around a corrected COI same-day if the building bounces the first one back.

## How Far Out Should You Start Planning an Office Move?

Eight to twelve weeks is a realistic floor for most small businesses, and that's before accounting for a managed building's paperwork timeline stacked on top. [Move.org's 2026 office moving checklist](https://www.move.org/office-moving-checklist/) recommends starting three to six months out for larger or more complex relocations, which lines up with what actually happens once IT infrastructure, vendor contracts, and a new lease all have to land on the same calendar.

| Timing | Task |
|---|---|
| 8-10 weeks out | Confirm new lease, appoint one internal move lead, get building COI requirements from both landlords |
| 6-8 weeks out | Book movers, confirm freight elevator windows, notify vendors of the address change |
| 3-4 weeks out | Back up all servers and workstations, order new furniture or fixtures, update signage and stationery |
| 1-2 weeks out | Employees pack personal desk items, confirm IT cutover plan, reconfirm elevator reservation |
| Move weekend | IT infrastructure moves first, then furniture and general contents |

One person needs to own this list. Splitting it across department heads is how a freight elevator reservation quietly falls through the cracks three weeks before move day.

## What Does an Office Move Cost in Boston?

Nationally, small business office moves run $2,000 to $10,000, according to Move.org's 2026 breakdown. Boston tends to land toward the higher half of that range, and not because the movers themselves cost more. Downtown commercial rents and building fees for after-hours freight elevator access, weekend labor rates, and any COI-related delays are what typically push the number up, not the physical distance between old and new addresses.

| Office Size | Typical Local Move Cost | Common Add-Ons |
|---|---|---|
| Small office (1-10 employees) | $2,000-$4,000 | After-hours labor, COI processing |

## Move Your IT Infrastructure First

Furniture is replaceable on short notice. A dead network connection on Monday morning isn't. Back up every server and workstation before anything gets unplugged, verify the backups actually restore rather than just exist, and get your new location's internet and phone lines live and tested before the first desk arrives. A comprehensive technology audit beforehand, what's moving, what's being retired, what depends on what, catches the surprises that otherwise surface at 7 a.m. Monday when nobody has time left to fix them.

Sequence the move so IT goes first: network gear, servers, and phone systems land and get tested at the new address while everything else is still being loaded at the old one. That way, if something needs troubleshooting, it happens with a full weekend of runway instead of an hour before employees walk in.

## Weekend, After-Hours, or Weekday: When Should You Move?

Weekday moves are cheaper, but they cost you a full business day, sometimes two once setup and troubleshooting are counted. For most small businesses, a Friday-evening-to-Monday-morning window is worth the higher labor rate. You keep the old office fully operational through Friday afternoon and have the new one live before Monday's first meeting.

This is where the building paperwork bites hardest. Weekend and after-hours freight elevator slots are the ones commercial buildings restrict the most, sometimes blocking them off entirely or charging a premium for staff coverage outside normal hours. If a weekend move matters to your business, get that request in with the property manager the same week you sign your new lease, not the week before you move.

We see this pattern on Boston office jobs regularly: the business that locks in its moving date and building paperwork early gets the weekend slot it wants. The one that waits ends up choosing between a weekday move it didn't want or whatever elevator time is still open.

## Don't Forget Employee Belongings and Sensitive Documents

Desks accumulate years of personal items nobody thinks about until move day. Have employees pack and label their own boxes a few days ahead rather than leaving it for the crew to sort out, which slows everything down and risks someone's belongings ending up in the wrong stack.

Sensitive material needs its own track entirely. Contracts, HR files, financial records, and anything under a client confidentiality agreement should move in locked bins with a documented chain of custody, not a generic banker's box labeled "misc." If your industry has specific document-handling rules, loop in whoever manages compliance before move day, not after a file goes missing in transit.

## Update Your Address and Vendor Contracts Before You Need To

The address change list for a business is longer than most owners expect going in, and it runs well past the obvious mail-forwarding step. Miss one line item and you're not just inconvenienced; a lender or vendor notice can go to an office you don't occupy anymore.

Start this list the same week you sign the new lease, not the week of the move. Assign it to whoever's already managing vendor relationships day to day, since they already know which contracts exist and won't need to go hunting for them under a deadline. A simple shared spreadsheet with a column for "notified" and "confirmed" catches the accounts people assume got updated but didn't. At minimum, that list should cover:

- USPS mail forwarding and business cards or letterhead
- Secretary of the Commonwealth filing and any state or local licenses tied to the address
- Banking, merchant services, and insurance policies
- Vendor contracts with delivery or service-address clauses
- Website, directory listings, and email signature footers

Signage is easy to forget until a client shows up at the old address on moving week. If your new location needs permitting for exterior signage, city approval can take longer than the move itself, so get that application filed as early in the process as your lease allows.

## Is an Office Move Tax-Deductible?

Generally, yes, and this is one place a business move works differently than [a personal relocation](/blog/relocation-package-boston-move). The [IRS treats ordinary and necessary business expenses](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/deducting-business-expenses) as deductible in the year they're incurred, and relocating your office, movers, transport, temporary storage, generally qualifies. There's a wrinkle worth flagging to your accountant: costs tied to installing newly purchased equipment usually get capitalized and depreciated instead of deducted outright, so keep moving costs and equipment purchase costs on separate line items when you're tracking receipts.

None of this replaces real tax advice specific to your business structure, but it's worth knowing the deduction exists before you assume a move is a pure cost with no offset.
