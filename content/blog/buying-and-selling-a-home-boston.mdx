You've got a closing on your old place at 9am and the keys to your new one by 2pm, maybe. That "maybe" is the entire problem, and it's why buying and selling a home at the same time is one of the hardest moves to plan on paper.

## The Three Ways to Sequence Buying and Selling at Once

Most people default to assuming they'll sell and buy on the same day, hand off keys in both directions, and move once. It's the cleanest outcome, and it happens less often than people expect.

There are really three ways this plays out. Sell first, then negotiate a rent-back from your buyer so you can stay in your old place a few extra days while your new purchase closes. Buy first, which means carrying two mortgages or a bridge loan for a short window but only moving once. Or make your purchase contingent on your sale closing, which protects you financially but weakens your offer in a competitive market like Boston's.

| Approach | How It Works | Best For | Biggest Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sell first, rent back | You close your sale, then pay the buyer to stay a few extra days | Sellers who need the sale proceeds for their down payment | Rent-back terms fall through or run short |
| Buy first | You close on the new home before selling the old one | Buyers who can qualify for two mortgages briefly | Carrying two housing payments longer than planned |
| Contingent offer | Your purchase only closes once your sale closes | Buyers who can't afford to carry two properties | Sellers often reject contingent offers outright |

None of these is objectively correct. They trade financial risk for moving-day complexity, or the reverse, and the right one depends on how much cash cushion you have and how competitive the specific Boston neighborhood is.

## Why Massachusetts Closings Work Differently

Here's the part most national moving guides get wrong when they talk about "closing day": they assume a title company handles everything remotely. Massachusetts isn't that kind of state. Closings here happen in person, at a table, run by a real estate attorney rather than a title company or escrow agent.

The timeline reflects that. After an accepted offer, both sides typically sign a purchase and sale agreement following a standard 72-hour attorney review period, and [the full process from accepted offer to closing usually runs four to six weeks](https://www.cote-law.com/real-estate-closing-process-in-massachusetts/). Every document, every signature, every wire transfer happens in that one room, on that one day, which is exactly why a single delayed piece of paperwork can push a closing from morning to late afternoon without much warning.

That matters for movers. A crew scheduled to arrive at noon assuming you'll have keys by then is a real gamble in a state where the whole transaction lives or dies at one table.

## What Happens When Your Dates Don't Line Up?

This is the scenario that actually derails people, more than the closing itself. Your sale closes Tuesday. Your purchase doesn't close until the following Monday. Where do you and your furniture go for six days?

The legal mechanism for bridging this in Massachusetts is a [use and occupancy agreement](https://www.pulgininorton.com/use-and-occupancy-agreements.html), which gives you a license to occupy a property before the deed formally transfers. It needs a specific, bounded date range rather than an open-ended one, and it typically comes with a daily fee held in escrow by the closing attorney to cover potential damage. Sellers are generally more comfortable letting a buyer move in early, or staying on after their own sale, when the arrangement is on paper with a hard end date rather than a handshake.

If a use and occupancy agreement isn't on the table, you're down to two options: pay for a few nights somewhere else, or put your belongings in storage and figure out where you're sleeping separately. Neither is fun, but storage is usually the more predictable and less expensive of the two once the gap runs more than two or three days.

## Should You Book Movers for Closing Day Itself?

Plenty of people do it and it works out fine. But [real estate professionals who see this go sideways tend to recommend scheduling the actual move one to three days after closing](https://www.amerisave.com/learn/buying-and-selling-a-home-simultaneously-your-complete-strategic-guide), not on it, precisely because possession doesn't always transfer at 9am the way the calendar implies.

If you're set on a same-day move, plan the truck for the afternoon, not the morning, and have a real backup for where your things go if the closing itself slides past 3 or 4pm. This is also where [booking further ahead than you think you need to](/blog/how-far-in-advance-to-book-movers-boston) actually pays off — a mover who already has your date and a flexible afternoon window is a lot easier to work with than one you're calling at 8am asking for a same-day rescue.

## Storage or Temporary Housing: Which Actually Works?

When the two closings genuinely won't line up, most people end up choosing between three fixes: a rent-back agreement, short-term storage, or temporary housing like a short-term rental or a relative's spare room.

Storage tends to win on cost and predictability. A month of [self-storage or a portable container in the Boston area](/blog/boston-storage-options-during-a-move) runs a fraction of what a week of temporary housing costs, and it removes the logistics of moving your stuff twice. The tradeoff is you're living without your furniture for however long the gap runs, which is fine for a week and genuinely uncomfortable past a month.

Temporary housing solves the comfort problem but adds cost and a second move — everything goes into a short-term rental, then gets moved again once your new place is ready. If you go this route, at minimum pack a separate set of essentials — clothes, toiletries, chargers, whatever the kids need — so you're not digging through storage boxes for a toothbrush.

## The Boston-Specific Complications

A double closing gets harder, not easier, once you factor in the city itself. If your purchase is a condo in a building with a shared freight elevator, most Boston buildings won't let a mover on site without a [certificate of insurance and an elevator reservation](/blog/boston-high-rise-moving-elevator-coi) booked weeks ahead. If your closing date shifts, that reservation shifts with it, and elevator windows during busy stretches of the year fill up fast.

Parking is its own problem. If either address is in a neighborhood with narrow streets or permit-only parking, your movers may need a [no-parking permit reserved with the city](/blog/boston-moving-truck-parking-permit) in advance, and permits are tied to a specific date. A closing that slips by even two days means refiling.

And if your closing happens to land anywhere near the end of August, you're competing with the single busiest moving stretch Boston sees all year, when a huge share of the city's leases turn over at once. Truck availability and elevator slots both get tighter than usual in that window, so build in extra lead time if your timeline overlaps it at all.

## Building a Realistic Budget and Timeline

The honest budgeting mistake in a simultaneous buy-sell is treating the move itself as the only cost. It rarely is. Storage fees, a rent-back payment, or a few nights in temporary housing all show up as line items people forget to plan for until they're already mid-move.

Give yourself real slack. Book your movers with a flexible window rather than a single fixed hour, confirm your use and occupancy terms in writing if there's any chance of a gap, and price out a month of storage even if you're hoping not to need it. It's easier to cancel a reservation you didn't end up needing than to scramble for one the week your closing slides.

This is exactly the kind of move where locking in your price at booking earns its keep. Closings shift for reasons that have nothing to do with you — a delayed mortgage payoff, a title search that turns something up, a scheduling backlog at the registry of deeds — and none of that should mean renegotiating your moving quote on top of everything else. When the closing date is the one thing you can't fully control, the price of the move shouldn't be a second variable in play.
