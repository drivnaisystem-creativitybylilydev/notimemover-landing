Federal rules stopped requiring an estimator on your couch years ago. Here's when a video walkthrough gives you a number that holds, and when it quietly sets you up for a bad surprise at the door.

## The Three Ways Movers Size a Move

Every quote you get comes from one of three methods, and the method matters more than the number.

An in-home estimate is the old standard: someone walks your apartment, opens closets, counts boxes, looks at your stairs, and writes a number. A virtual survey does the same thing through your phone camera, either on a live video call or a recording you make yourself and send in. An instant online quote skips inspection entirely and prices off whatever you type into a form — square footage, bedroom count, a checkbox or two.

| Estimate Method | Typical Time | Best For | Main Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| In-home walkthrough | 30-60 min, plus scheduling | 3+ bedrooms, specialty items, complex access | Hard to schedule in peak season |
| Virtual survey (video) | 10-30 min, often same-day | Studios through 2BR, standard furniture | Missed rooms, poor lighting, skipped access |
| Instant online form | Under 5 min | Ballpark budgeting only | No inventory means no accountability |

The first two produce a documented inventory. The third produces a guess with your name on it. That distinction is the whole article.

## What Federal Rules Actually Require

This is where most guides get vague, so here's the specific rule. Under [49 CFR 375.401](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/49/375.401), a mover handling an interstate move has to base its written estimate on a survey of your household goods when those goods sit within a 50-mile radius of the agent preparing the estimate. You can waive that survey, but you have to do it in writing, and the waiver gets kept as an addendum to your bill of lading.

Video counts. FMCSA's [April 2022 final rule implementing the Household Goods Working Group recommendations](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2022/04/26/2022-08808/implementation-of-household-goods-working-group-recommendations) formally allowed virtual surveys and extended the survey requirement beyond the 50-mile radius. A survey done virtually uses live or pre-recorded video, so long as the mover can clearly identify what's being transported.

Two practical takeaways. A mover offering a video survey instead of a house visit is following the rules, not cutting a corner. And a mover offering a firm interstate price with no survey of any kind, no video, no inventory list, is asking you to waive something — which is a very different conversation than "we do everything online now."

A purely local move inside Massachusetts sits outside those federal rules and gets quoted on hours, crew, and inventory rather than shipment weight. The underlying question survives the jurisdiction change: is this number fixed, or is it a starting point? Our breakdown of [binding versus non-binding quotes](/blog/binding-vs-non-binding-moving-quotes) covers how to ask that so you get a real answer.

## Is a Virtual Estimate as Accurate as an In-Home Estimate?

Honest answer: it depends far less on the format than on how carefully the walkthrough gets done.

A rushed in-home visit where the estimator never opens the hall closet produces a worse inventory than a careful fifteen-minute video where you narrate every room. A thorough in-home visit beats a shaky phone tour of a dark apartment. Format sets the ceiling; effort determines where you land under it.

Where in-person still has a real edge is volume and judgment at scale. Three-bedroom houses, packed basements, and anything requiring custom crating are genuinely hard to size through a screen, because the estimator can't lift a lid or read how deep a stack goes. Under roughly two bedrooms of standard furniture, that edge mostly disappears.

One rule matters more than picking a side: compare like with like. Three video-survey quotes can be read against each other, because all three movers worked from the same kind of information. One in-home estimate stacked against two numbers someone guessed over the phone tells you almost nothing, since the phone numbers were never based on an inventory in the first place. Get several quotes, and ask each company which method produced theirs before you line them up.

## When a Video Walkthrough Is Enough

Most Boston apartment moves land squarely in video territory. A studio, a one-bedroom, a two-bedroom with a normal amount of furniture and no piano — a good estimator can size that from a ten-minute recording without breaking a sweat.

Video is also the practical answer in three situations where an in-home visit barely works at all:

- You're moving into Boston from out of state and nobody can walk the origin apartment anyway.
- You're booking inside a tight window. Around [September 1](/blog/moving-september-1-boston), estimators are booked solid, and holding a quote for a scheduling slot you can't get is how people end up with no mover at all.
- Your building makes visits awkward. Some managed high-rises want a certificate of insurance on file before anyone from a moving company sets foot inside, which turns a simple estimate into paperwork.

## When Should You Insist on an In-Home Estimate?

Push for an in-home visit when the move has real money or real weight on the line.

Three or more bedrooms is the usual threshold, mostly because volume errors compound. A piano, a gun safe, a pool table, a marble slab, or a full workshop deserves eyes on it, since [oversized specialty items](/blog/moving-pianos-safes-pool-tables-boston) are priced on equipment and crew rather than cubic feet. Long-distance moves priced by weight are worth the visit too, because a bad volume estimate on an [interstate move out of Boston](/blog/moving-out-of-state-from-boston) turns into a bad weight estimate, which turns into a real number at delivery.

And if you're the kind of person who'd rather point at something and ask than film it and hope — that's a legitimate reason on its own. A good mover will send someone.

## What Do Boston Estimators Miss on a Video Call?

Almost never the furniture. It's the access.

Here's the pattern we see constantly on Boston jobs: the inventory is fine, and the thing that changes the day is the walk. A triple-decker in Somerville or Dorchester where the stairs turn twice and the second landing won't clear a sofa without standing it on end. A Beacon Hill street where the truck parks a hundred feet down and every box gets carried. A basement storage bin in an Allston building that nobody mentioned because it didn't feel like part of the apartment.

None of that shows up in a video of your living room. All of it shows up in the hours. If you've read our [neighborhood-by-neighborhood difficulty guide](/blog/hardest-boston-neighborhoods-to-move), you already know how much variance those factors carry across the city.

We build every NoTimeMover quote off the inventory and access details a customer gives us upfront, and then we lock the budget before the crew shows up. That model only works when the picture is complete. The single most useful thing you can do on any walkthrough, ours or anyone else's, is show the parts of the job that aren't furniture.

## How to Film a Walkthrough That Actually Holds Up

Fifteen minutes of decent footage is worth more than an hour of a scheduling back-and-forth. Do it in daylight with the lights on, walk slowly, and narrate as you go.

Cover all of it:

- Every room, including the ones you're "mostly not taking." Estimators price what they see.
- Inside closets, under beds, and on top of wardrobes. Open the doors.
- Basement bins, attic space, garage, and any shared storage in the building.
- The stairs, filmed from the bottom up, including every landing and turn.
- The front door, hallway width, and elevator if there is one.
- The street outside, so the mover can see where a truck would fit and how far the carry runs.

Say out loud what you don't want moved, and say what you're adding — the mattress arriving next week, the sectional you're buying, the boxes you haven't packed yet. Those change a quote, and they change it far less painfully before the estimate than after. Getting this right up front is also the cleanest way to avoid the [add-on fees that show up on move day](/blog/hidden-moving-fees-boston).

## What About Instant Online Quotes?

They're fine for budgeting and useless as a commitment.

A form-based number tells you roughly what a two-bedroom [local move](/local-moving) costs in this market, which is genuinely useful when you're deciding whether to hire movers at all. It doesn't survive contact with your actual apartment, because nothing in that form knows about your stairs, your storage bin, or the three-flight carry to the truck.

The line to watch: when an instant quote gets presented as a locked price with no inventory behind it. That's the lowball pattern our guide on [choosing a Boston moving company](/blog/how-to-choose-a-moving-company-boston) covers in detail, and it's the reason to treat "no inspection needed" as a question rather than a convenience.
