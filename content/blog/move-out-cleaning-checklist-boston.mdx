Massachusetts law says your landlord can't touch your deposit over normal wear and tear. A dirty apartment is a different story, and it's the easiest way to lose money you're otherwise entitled to.

## What Massachusetts Law Actually Requires

Your lease, not state law, is where the cleaning obligation actually comes from. Most Boston leases require you to return the unit in the condition you received it, ordinary wear and tear excepted, and "ordinary wear and tear" is doing a lot of work in that sentence.

Here's what's actually enforceable. Under [Massachusetts General Law c. 186 § 15B](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/find-out-what-landlords-can-use-security-deposits-for), landlords cannot deduct from your security deposit for normal wear and tear, only for unpaid rent, unpaid water charges, or documented damage. They also can't charge you a mandatory cleaning fee as a lease condition upfront. What they can legally do is hire a cleaner after you leave and deduct the actual cost from your deposit, with an itemized, signed statement, if the unit genuinely wasn't left clean.

That last part is where most people lose money. Not from some illegal fee, but from a legitimate, documented cleaning bill that gets subtracted because the stove was never wiped down.

## Should You Clean Before or After the Truck Is Loaded?

After, every time. Cleaning around furniture and boxes is slower and worse: you skip the floor under the couch, you wipe counters that get re-dirtied by packing tape and dust an hour later, and you're doing the work twice.

We see this constantly on Boston jobs. Once the truck is loaded and the apartment is empty, everything that was hidden becomes visible at once. Dust bunnies behind the fridge, scuff marks the couch was covering, a baseboard nobody's touched in two years. Budget 2-4 hours after the last box leaves for a studio or one-bedroom, more for anything larger. If your [move is scheduled for a specific window](/local-moving), build the cleaning time into that window rather than assuming you'll "get to it after."

There's one exception. Wiping out cabinet interiors and drawers can happen earlier, as you empty each one, since that work doesn't depend on the furniture being gone. Save the floors, baseboards, and appliance exteriors for last, once nothing's left to re-dirty them.

## The Room-by-Room Move-Out Cleaning Checklist

Landlords and cleaning services both work off some version of this list. Kitchens and bathrooms get the closest inspection, since that's where uncleanliness shows up fastest and where most deposit deductions originate.

| Room | Key Tasks | Time (empty apt) |
|---|---|---|
| Kitchen | Degrease stovetop/hood, clean inside fridge and oven, wipe cabinet interiors, scrub sink | 45-75 min |
| Bathroom | Scrub tub/shower grout, disinfect toilet base, descale faucets, clean mirror and vanity | 30-45 min |
| Bedrooms | Vacuum/mop floors, wipe baseboards and window sills, clean closet shelving | 15-25 min each |
| Living areas | Vacuum/mop, wipe light switches and outlet covers, clean window tracks | 20-30 min |
| Common | Walls (spot clean, not repaint), doors and door frames, light fixtures | 20-40 min |

Appliances are worth double-checking. An oven with baked-on grease or a fridge with old spills is the single most common source of a real cleaning deduction, more than walls or floors.

## What Counts as Normal Wear and Tear vs. Damage?

This distinction is the whole ballgame, and it's genuinely worth understanding before you argue with a landlord about a deduction. Wear and tear is what happens from ordinary living: faded paint, slightly worn carpet paths, loose door hinges, minor scuffs at chair-rail height. It's not chargeable, full stop.

Damage is different. Large stains that won't lift, cracked tile, holes in drywall beyond small nail holes, pet damage to flooring, broken fixtures. That's chargeable, and reasonably so. The gray area is grime: a genuinely dirty stovetop or bathroom isn't wear and tear, it's a cleaning obligation you didn't meet, and it gets billed as such. If you're unsure which category something falls into, [Apartments.com's move-out guide](https://www.apartments.com/blog/move-out-cleaning-checklist) has useful photos of what inspectors flag most often.

Older Boston buildings blur this line more than newer construction does. Radiator rust marks on a windowsill, paint chipping near a century-old sash window, a slightly uneven hardwood floor, these are usually wear and tear in a pre-war triple-decker, not something you caused. A reasonable landlord knows the difference between an old building aging and a tenant who trashed the place; an unreasonable one will try to charge you for both.

## Hire a Cleaner or DIY It? Boston Costs Compared

Both work. The math depends on your unit size and how much time you actually have left before keys are due.

| Apartment Size | Professional Cleaning | DIY Time (2 people) |
|---|---|---|
| Studio | $150-$800 | 2-3 hours |
| 1BR | $200-$1,000 | 3-4 hours |
| 2BR | $250-$1,400 | 4-6 hours |

Those professional ranges reflect Boston-specific 2026 pricing data from [Care.com and Homeyou](https://www.care.com/cost/move-out-cleaning-services/boston-ma). A full move-in/move-out clean with inside-cabinet and appliance detailing, the level most leases actually expect, tends to land at $280-$500 for an average one- or two-bedroom.

DIY makes sense if your move date leaves you a spare afternoon and the unit isn't in rough shape. Hiring makes more sense the week of September 1, when everyone's schedule is compressed at once and a professional crew can do in three hours what would take you an exhausted evening.

## What Triple-Deckers and Walk-Ups Get Missed

Multi-family buildings add a layer most cleaning checklists skip entirely. If you're in a classic Boston triple-decker or a walk-up in Allston, Somerville, or JP, your unit isn't the only thing getting inspected.

Hallway walls and stairwells take real damage during a move, scuffed corners, a scraped baseboard from a dresser that didn't quite clear the turn, and landlords in owner-occupied triple-deckers notice this fast since they live in the building. A quick wipe-down of the common stairwell you used isn't strictly your unit's responsibility, but it's a five-minute goodwill gesture that heads off a dispute before it starts. Check your unit's door frame and threshold too; door damage from moving furniture is one of the most commonly disputed charges we hear about from Boston renters.

Narrow stairwells are also where most accidental scuffs happen in the first place, not inside the unit itself. Corner guards or a moving blanket taped over a tight stairwell turn take two minutes to set up and prevent the exact damage that ends up as a disputed line item later. It's a cheap trade against a wall-repair deduction that's entirely avoidable.

## Document Everything Before You Hand Over the Keys

Take photos and a walkthrough video of every room once cleaning is done, before you hand back keys or leave for the final walkthrough. Open cabinets and appliance doors in the video so the inside is visible too, not just the exterior. Timestamp them if your phone doesn't do it automatically. This is your evidence if a deduction shows up later that doesn't match what you actually left behind.

This step matters more than it sounds like it should. Massachusetts law gives your landlord 30 days to return your deposit with an itemized list if they're withholding any of it, and disputes tend to come down to your word against theirs unless you documented the condition yourself. If you took a similar walkthrough video when you first moved in, pull it up and compare the two side by side. It's the fastest way to prove which marks were already there before you signed the lease.

## What If Your Landlord Still Withholds the Deposit?

Cleaning well protects you, but it doesn't guarantee a fast, full refund. If your landlord misses the 30-day window, doesn't provide an itemized list, or deducts for something that's clearly wear and tear, that's a separate legal process from the cleaning itself.
